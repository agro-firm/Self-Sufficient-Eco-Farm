# 1. Final Vision

This farm is not only a crop farm. It is a complete **self-sufficient production campus** where:

- crops produce human food and animal feed;
- livestock produce milk, eggs, youngstock and manure;
- manure becomes biogas and fertilizer;
- solar + biogas provide energy;
- the perimeter canal provides fish, stored water, flood buffering and fire water;
- harvested crops are milled, dried, cooled and stored on-site;
- human wastewater, animal wastewater, processing wastewater and oily workshop water are treated separately;
- clean family areas, clean production areas, dirty/service areas and industrial areas remain physically and operationally separated;
- roads, CCTV, lighting, emergency access, quarantine and utility systems are fully integrated.

## 1.1 Core Principles

1. **One ecosystem, but not one mixed space.**
2. Waste from one section becomes an input for another section only after safe handling or treatment.
3. No raw sewage or raw animal wastewater enters the fish canal.
4. Animal numbers follow proven feed capacity.
5. Processing and storage are essential infrastructure, not optional extras.
6. Self-sufficiency means resilience and low dependency, not unsafe “zero purchase.”
7. Rooftop solar is preferred before using prime agricultural land.
8. Crop sunlight must not be blocked by tall trees, buildings or utility structures.
9. Clean and dirty traffic routes remain separate.
10. Critical utilities are placed above design flood level.
11. Emergency access remains available from two gates.
12. All major resource loops are measured with KPIs so the farm expands only when capacity is proven.

## Placement Logic

- Food crops stay in the most open solar-protected area.
- Tall orchard trees are kept outside the crop solar envelope.
- Fodder is located close to the ruminant district.
- Livestock, manure handling and biogas stay close to the service side.
- The processing hub sits where crop and service routes meet efficiently.
- House/playground remain away from dirty traffic and industrial noise.
- Energy/water controls remain centrally accessible and above flood level.

# 7. Crop Sunlight and Shadow Control

- No tall mango, coconut or timber rows immediately beside open-field crops.
- No large buildings on the low-sun side of critical vegetable blocks without a shadow study.
- Orchard trees remain grouped in a dedicated block instead of being scattered through crop land.
- Solar arrays stay on roofs before occupying prime agricultural land.
- Final sun/shadow analysis should use exact north orientation, mature tree heights and building heights.

## 41.1 Canal Volume Check

For a 250×220 m rectangle, perimeter = **940 m**.

At about 6 m clear-water width and 1.5–2.0 m average operating depth, theoretical gross canal volume is approximately **8,460–11,280 m³**. After accounting for bridges, bank geometry, structures and freeboard, a planning working range of **about 8,000–10,500 m³** is reasonable for concept work.

This is **not** an excavation quantity. Final survey and cross-sections are required.

# 47. Biogas Capacity Verification

Earlier planning used a **20–25 m³/day class biogas system** as a feasibility starting point.

Because the final self-reliance base herd is intentionally smaller, actual gas production may be lower unless:
- more manure is available;
- controlled poultry litter is co-digested;
- approved kitchen/crop organic waste is added.

Final digester sizing must use:
1. daily manure kg;
2. total solids;
3. added water;
4. hydraulic retention time;
5. temperature;
6. daily gas demand.

The **25–40 kW biogas generator concept does not mean continuous full-load operation**. Generator dispatch hours must match actual gas production and storage.
