# Internal Access Spines, Biosecurity Buffers, Headlands & Swales — Detailed Description

## What this space does

- internal access
- biosecurity separation
- headlands/turning
- vegetated swales
- fire breaks
- utility corridors

## Why it is placed here

This is intentionally distributed rather than one rectangle. It makes the internal geometry operationally usable without stealing uncounted land.

## Interfaces

### Required / useful neighbors
- Touches all three internal horizontal bands
- Connects to main clean gate
- Interfaces with zone headlands

### Controlled / prohibited interfaces
- being consumed by decorative landscaping
- unallocated buildings
- blocked emergency route
- dirty/clean crossing

## Utility dependencies

- internal roads
- swales
- pedestrian separation
- utility corridors
- turning/buffer areas

## Drainage / wastewater principle

Swales and clean runoff controls may occupy this reserve; dirty wastewater does not.

## Main risks

- loss of allocated area to unrelated uses
- cross-contamination between clean and dirty systems
- flood/cyclone damage
- poor maintenance access
- unplanned utility crossings
- future expansion without feed/water/energy/waste capacity checks

## Verification checklist

- [ ] Area remains within parent allocation
- [ ] L1 position is unchanged
- [ ] Required access is clear
- [ ] Drainage path is explicit
- [ ] Power/water/data needs are explicit
- [ ] Fire/emergency access is explicit
- [ ] Neighbor conflicts are checked
- [ ] Image/blueprint matches the master plan
